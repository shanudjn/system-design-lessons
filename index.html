System Design

One concrete system per lesson, traced end to end. Estimate → model → trace the paths → find the first bottleneck.

LESSON 12
Indexing & Search: How a Database Finds One Row in 100 Million
One 100M-row orders table, three questions. Price the no-index baseline (a 20 GB scan = 40 s) and watch an index cut it to ~0.4 ms; build a B-tree from page size (8 KB ÷ 16 B ≈ 500 fan-out → just 3 levels); trace a point lookup, a secondary index's hidden second hop (fixed by a covering index), a range query, and full-text "blue running shoes" via an inverted index. Then flip it: when an index hurts — the 6× write tax and the low-selectivity index that's 125× slower than a scan.
LESSON 11
CAP in Practice: What a Partition Forces You to Give Up
One account balance ($100), three replicas, and a cut trans-atlantic cable. See why partition tolerance isn't optional (so CAP is really "C or A, only while split"); recover the quorum rule W+R>N; then watch the same partition push a CP store to refuse a write (errors, but no double-spend) and an AP store to accept both sides (a $60,000 reconciliation bill — right for a cart, wrong for a balance). Ends on the choice CAP hides: PACELC's ~90 ms everyday latency-vs-consistency tax.
LESSON 10
Leader Election & Coordination: One Leader, No Split-Brain
Five nodes must run a billing job exactly once. Size the failover clock (1 s heartbeat, 3 s timeout, ~3.5 s failover) and see why a too-short timeout makes leadership flap; learn terms and one-vote-per-term; watch a majority make two leaders mathematically impossible when the network splits (two groups of 3 in a set of 5 must overlap); fence the zombie leader by epoch; and learn why coordination clusters run 3, 5, or 7 — never 6.
LESSON 09
Message Queues: The Log, Exactly-Once & Ordering
One 100k-events/sec click stream through a log-based queue. Size it (20 MB/s, 12 TB, 20 partitions), see why a log keeps data and each reader tracks its own offset, walk the three delivery semantics (at-most-once loses, at-least-once duplicates, "exactly-once" = at-least-once + idempotency), then partition-by-key for ordering — and pay for it with a hot partition, consumer lag, and the poison message a DLQ quarantines.
LESSON 08
Rate Limiting: Token Bucket, Leaky Bucket & Distributed Counters
One runaway API key, gated fairly. Size the limit (sustained rate + burst), watch the fixed-window boundary bug leak 2× traffic, walk the token bucket (burst then clamp) against the leaky bucket (smooth drip), then fix the 10× per-server leak with a centralized atomic counter — and shard or approximate when that store becomes the wall.
LESSON 07
Designing for Failure: Timeouts, Retries, Idempotency & Backpressure
One timed-out call, traced end to end. A timeout isn't an answer (partial failure), so set it from p99 not a lazy 1 s default, make the retry safe with an idempotency key, gentle with backoff + jitter + a retry budget, and cut losses with a circuit breaker — then backpressure and load shedding instead of an unbounded queue.
LESSON 06
Consistency & Replication: A Like-Counter Under Concurrent Writes
A viral link takes 50,000 likes/sec and the count goes up by one, not two — the lost update. Atomic increment to fix the write, replication to scale reads, replication lag and read-your-writes, strong vs eventual, quorums (W+R>N), and the sharded counter.
LESSON 05
Async Work: Queues, Workers, and the 2-Million-Link Job
A bulk job that would run 2.8 hours inline — pushed onto a queue and drained by workers in ~8 minutes. Visibility timeouts, at-least-once vs idempotency, poison messages + dead-letter queues, ordering by partition, and backpressure.
LESSON 04
Consistent Hashing: Add a Shard Without the Storm
Adding a 17th shard moved 94% of keys — here's the circle that drops it to ~6%. The hash ring, clockwise ownership, and virtual nodes for even load and graceful failure.
LESSON 03
Scaling Writes: One Counter, Many Shards
When 40,000 writes/sec break the single ID counter and the single disk: distributed ID generation (ID blocks vs Snowflake), hash vs range sharding, and why adding a shard reshuffles 94% of keys.
LESSON 02
Scaling Reads: Eviction, CDNs, and the Stampede
Why a 1% cache serves 96% of reads, LRU vs TTL eviction, the cache stampede (thundering herd) and how to stop it, then CDNs and the speed of light.

Design a URL Shortener
Estimation, data model, counter+Base62 codes, read/write paths, 301 vs 302, and the first bottleneck → caching.
