**Date:** 2026-08-02

**Curriculum spine item:** #46 (Distributed job scheduling)
**Worked example:** the platform's scheduler — 1,000,000 registered jobs (subscription renewals, nightly reports, 5-min sweeps, trial reminders), firing ~2,000,000 times/day ≈ 23 fires/s average but wildly spiky.

## What this lesson covered

The framing: Lesson 45 delivered events the instant they happened; this lesson delivers them ON A CLOCK. A scheduled job is work bound to a time, and firing a million of them across a fleet breaks on two words that fight — **once** (never double-charge a renewal) and **on time** (3 AM means 3 AM). Followed the four-move spine:

- **Estimate** — one `cron` box fails three ways: (1) SPOF (L07) — a midnight outage silently drops every midnight fire, with no retry because the retrier is the thing that's down; a missed *schedule* is a fire that never happened, invisible until a customer notices. (2) Throughput ceiling — 200,000 jobs written `0 0 * * *` come due in one tick; at 0.5 ms/fire, 200,000 × 0.5 ms = **100 s** serial → two minutes late on the happy path with no failure. (3) The herd — 200,000 / 23 ≈ **~8,600×** the average rate in one second, born of humans rounding to `0 0` (L02/L07 thundering herd). Fix, twice: shard the dispatcher (L03/04/10) for availability + throughput; hand work to a queue + worker fleet (L05/09) so the tick never runs a job inline. NAMED trade: distributing buys "on time" but reintroduces the double-fire — the rest of the lesson buys "once" back.
- **Model** — firing safety across machines:
  - **Dispatcher vs worker (L05/09)** — the tick must be fast/predictable; if it *ran* jobs, one 40 s report would stall the clock for all (L09 head-of-line, L45 inline-delivery trap recostumed). Dispatcher only enqueues `{job_id, scheduled_time}`; workers execute + autoscale into the burst (L27/28).
  - **Shard the schedule (L03/04)** — partition by hash(job_id) into 16 shards; midnight batch → 200,000/16 = 12,500/shard × 0.5 ms = **6.25 s** (16× cut), a dead dispatcher stalls only its shard. Per-shard **lease** (L22) for failover (L10); consistent hashing → only ~1/N shards reshuffle on churn (L04).
  - **Atomic claim (L06 CAS)** — conditional `UPDATE ... WHERE id=... AND next_fire_time<=now() AND (owner IS NULL OR lease_until<now())`; of two racing dispatchers exactly one gets rows_affected=1. The `lease_until` bounds a crashed owner; the `epoch` that bumps per claim is a **fencing token** (L10/22). Beats "elect one global leader" (L10) which re-creates the single-box ceiling + failover gap — sharded leases give one owner PER SHARD.
  - **Idempotent job (L13)** — the hard truth: no lease makes double-fire impossible. Owner claims (30 s lease), enqueues, then GC-pauses 40 s (L22); lease expires, standby reclaims + re-enqueues → two runs. A lease is a **liveness** knob, never safety. Only defense = idempotent job keyed on (job_id, scheduled_time), effect recorded in the same txn (L13/33). **Exactly-once *attempts* impossible; exactly-once *effect* lives in the job** — L45's shape (at-least-once on the wire, idempotency at the destination) pointed at the clock. NAMED trade: lease length = false double-fire vs slow failover; tune lease for liveness, put correctness in idempotent jobs.
- **Trace** — (A) clean fire: tick → index range-read due slice (L12) → atomic claim (epoch 57) → enqueue → reschedule next_fire_time → worker idempotency-checks, charges, records key in same txn → DONE once. (B) double-fire race: two dispatchers UPDATE the same row → DB serializes (L06), rows_affected 1 vs 0 → one run. (B') GC-pause double-fire: two runs → worker idempotency key makes the 2nd a no-op — lease bounds liveness, idempotent job guarantees effect, both needed. (C) crash-then-recover: cluster down 00:00–00:11 → 200,000 jobs overdue → per-JOB policy: **coalesce** (send 1 daily digest, not the missed + current) vs **backfill** (each missed billing hour must run or revenue is silently skipped); catch-up rate-limited + jittered (L07/08) or it's the midnight herd again on recovery (L07 metastable), and idempotent (L13) so re-running catch-up is harmless.
- **First bottleneck** — the herd is in the SCHEDULE, not the scheduler (more dispatchers don't help — 200,000 jobs are genuinely due at once and dump 200,000 runs on downstreams together). Fix = **jitter**: deterministic offset hash(job_id) mod 60 s → 200,000/60 ≈ **3,333/s** over a minute instead of an 8,600× wall (L07/26/45). NAMED trade: timeliness precision vs smooth load — nightly jobs don't need sub-minute precision; the few that do (market-open) you provision for the spike. Second wall = **clock skew** (L35): every claim asks `next_fire_time <= now()` but no two wall clocks agree → (1) compare due-ness/lease-expiry against ONE authoritative clock (datastore's now()), not each node's wall clock (L35 "don't sort by local wall time"); (2) don't promise precision finer than NTP skew (a 1 s tick can't honestly distinguish sub-second); (3) leases expire on real elapsed time — a fencing token orders claims without a clock, but the timeout itself is a physical duration; logical clocks can't tell you 30 s passed (L35's closing caution).

Four traps: one scheduler / running jobs inline in the tick; assuming a lease gives exactly-once firing; no missed-fire policy (silent drop or replay-storm); the midnight herd + trusting local clocks.

Arithmetic double-checked: 2,000,000 fires/day ÷ 86,400 s = 23.15/s; 200,000 ÷ 23.15 = 8,639 ≈ ~8,600×; 200,000 × 0.5 ms = 100,000 ms = 100 s; 200,000/16 = 12,500 × 0.5 ms = 6,250 ms = 6.25 s; 200,000/60 = 3,333/s.
